2. The short version

  • You don’t need an account. We never ask for your name or email address.
  • The app has no ads, uses no advertising ID and doesn’t ask for your location.
  • To play the radio, your device connects directly to the laut.fm server. We don’t run that server and don’t see this connection data ourselves.
  • Favorites, volume and settings stay on your device.

This version of the app sends no usage statistics and no error data.

3. Data on your device (Section 25 TDDDG)

The app keeps small amounts of data locally on your device, or in your browser storage in the web version. That covers: your last station, your favorites (station, artist, title, time), volume, settings (for example scrolling text, appearance, sleep timer, alarm, autostart), the confirmation of the storage notice and, if asked, your choice on statistics and error reports. Cached track information and covers make the app start faster. If you turn off “Load covers”, the app remembers that even without a confirmed storage notice. Otherwise the next start would send requests to Apple again. This one privacy setting is therefore technically necessary (Section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). The same applies to the app language: if you choose it yourself under Settings > Language, the app saves your choice immediately and permanently, even without a confirmed storage notice. Otherwise you would have to set it again at every start. That is technically necessary so that the service you explicitly asked for runs in your language (Section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). Without a choice of your own, the app follows your device language and stores nothing for it.

For Google Cast, the web version stores in your browser storage (localStorage, key “linefm.googleCast”) whether you agreed to or declined loading the Cast script. This too is strictly necessary (Section 25(2) No. 2 TDDDG) so that a no is respected.

We need this data for the features you use yourself: remembering favorites, finding your station again, keeping your volume. We never receive it, and it contains no identifier that lets us recognise you. On Android it is excluded from Google backup and device transfer. On iPhone, iPad and Mac it can end up in a backup you create yourself with Apple (iCloud) or on your computer; we have no access to it. The legal basis is Section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG and, where the data is personal, Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR.

On first launch the app shows a notice about this storage on every platform. It is for your information. You can remove everything at any time under Settings > Clear storage. Your chosen app language is removed as well. Only the setting “Load covers: off” is kept, so the app sends no requests to Apple afterwards; you switch it back on with the switch itself. Uninstalling the app or clearing your browser data removes everything.

4. Radio stream and track information (laut.fm)

LINEFM is broadcast through the platform laut.fm run by LAUT AG. When you start a station, your device loads the stream straight from laut.fm. The same goes for track information, listener count and the live notice when a track changes (api.laut.fm). laut.fm receives the technical data it needs for this: your IP address, date and time, the requested address and details about your device and software. The radio can’t play without this connection. We don’t run that server and don’t see this data ourselves.

The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (you request the service), alternatively (f). laut.fm is responsible for this processing itself. How laut.fm handles it is described in its privacy policy:

laut.fm/datenschutz

5. Album covers (Apple iTunes Search API)

The app looks for a cover for each track. Your device queries Apple’s iTunes Search API (itunes.apple.com) and loads the image from Apple servers. Apple receives artist and title as the search term, your IP address and details about your device and software. If nothing is found, the app shows the station logo.

The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. Our interest: the player should show the matching cover. You can switch the loading off: turn off Settings > “Load covers”. The app then sends no requests to Apple. It shows the cover that laut.fm supplies with the track and, if there is none, the station logo. Your right to object (see “Your rights”) applies in addition. Apple Inc. is based in the USA, see “Recipients and transfers to third countries”.

6. Logos (cdn.linefm.de, radiogruppe.de)

The app loads station logos from cdn.linefm.de, operated by {{CDN_ANBIETER}}. This briefly creates server log data (IP address, time, requested file). The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR: we want to deliver the logos securely and quickly.

The app loads the logo of RadioGruppe Deutschland from radiogruppe.de. The operators of that site receive technically necessary data: your IP address, the time and the requested file. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR: we want to show the logo of the app’s provider. We have no influence on the processing there; their privacy policy applies.

7. Casting to other devices (Chromecast, AirPlay, Android Auto, CarPlay)

The Google Cast SDK only becomes active when you tap the Cast button in the app for the first time. Only then does the app look for Chromecast devices on your local network. There is one technical exception on Android: the Cast plugin we use may initialise the SDK when the app closes, even if you never used Cast. On Android this runs through the Google Cast SDK of Google Play services, on iOS through the Google Cast SDK for iOS (both Google Ireland Limited, Google LLC). On iOS the system asks before the first search whether the app may access the local network. When you cast, the app sends the stream address, title, artist, station name and the address of the cover (or the station logo) to the chosen device. The device then loads the stream from laut.fm and the image from the image server (Apple or the station) itself. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR, because you start the casting.

According to Google, the Google Cast SDK itself sends usage data to Google, for example about the Cast session and the device. Google describes how it processes this data in its privacy policy. The legal basis for our part is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR: we want casting to work reliably. You can object by not using Cast. The app then does not start the SDK on its own; the exception on Android when closing is described above. Google LLC is based in the USA, see “Recipients and transfers to third countries”.

policies.google.com/privacy

Web version (only with consent): in the browser, Cast works only in Chrome and Edge. The first time you tap the Cast button there, the page asks whether it may load a script from Google for this. Only after you say yes does it load the Cast script from www.gstatic.com (Google Ireland Limited). Google then receives your IP address and technical details about your browser and device. The browser itself shows you the device picker. While casting, the stream address, title, artist, station name and cover go to the device you picked. The device loads Google’s playback app and the stream directly from laut.fm. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG). This question is separate from audience measurement. You can withdraw your consent at any time under Settings > “Google Cast (Chromecast)”. If the script was already loaded, the page reloads once so that nothing from Google stays active.

AirPlay, Android Auto and CarPlay are operating system features (Apple, Google). The app only hands the system station, title and cover to display. We receive no data from this.

8. Notifications, background playback and alarm

Lock screen media controls and the alarm run entirely on your device. No push messages come from a server. Your operating system asks for permission for notifications, exact alarms and showing the alarm over the lock screen. You can withdraw these in the system settings at any time.

9. Sharing and external links

When you share a song or export favorites, the app creates the text only at that moment and hands it to your device’s share feature. You choose the recipients.

Links such as “Open song in Apple Music, Spotify or YouTube Music”, linefm.de or radiogruppe.de open in your browser or the respective app only after you tap them. For the music services, artist and title go along as a search term. After that the provider’s own privacy policy applies.

Local data stays until you delete it or remove the app. Server logs of the web version are kept for {{LOG_AUFBEWAHRUNG}}. laut.fm, Apple and Google apply their own retention periods.

13. Your rights

You have the right of access (Art. 15 GDPR), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and objection (Art. 21). Where processing is based on consent, you can withdraw it at any time with effect for the future (Art. 7(3)). Where we process data based on legitimate interests, you can object on grounds relating to your particular situation.

Write to us at the email address in section 1. Since we don’t know you through an account, we can only link data on your device to you if you show it to us. Most of it you can delete directly in the app (Settings > Clear storage).

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77 GDPR). The one responsible for us: {{AUFSICHTSBEHOERDE_DATENSCHUTZ}}.

14. Obligation to provide data, automated decisions

You don’t have to provide any personal data to use the app. Only the connection to laut.fm is technically required. There is no automated decision-making, including profiling, under Art. 22 GDPR.
